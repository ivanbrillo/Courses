Makes use of experimental collections

  • Documents (corpora)

    • They are not query! We could have a topic and one or more associated query
    • Typically they are 50 → statistically significant
    • They are composed of:
      • title: brief statement expressing info need → used as query (resembles the typical search engine query)
      • description: more detailed formulation of info need
      • narrative: instructions for assessors on when to consider a document relevant
  • Relevance judgments (binary or graded) also called relevance assessment or ground-truth (or qrels)
    • 4 elements per row (tab or space separated):
      • topic id: corresponding id of the topic
      • fixed (ignored)
      • document no. (identify document univocally in the collection) [string]
      • judgement (binary or graded) → not all documents are judged (impossible) [typically 1k per topic] → assumed that all the others are not relevant
        • Pooling: top kkk﻿ results (between 50 and 200) from the rankings obtained by different search engines are merged into a pool, then duplicates are removed and presented in some random order to the relevance assessors to avoid bias

This ensures comparability and repeatability of the experiments (if not replicable wouldn’t be science)

User Model

Rank-based evaluation measures, implicitly or explicitly, embed a user model (can be more or less correlated with user behaviours) comprising:

  1. A browsing model that describes how a user interacts with results
    • In case of DCG: user steps down the ranked results one-by-one, until he reaches the stopping rank kkk﻿ which is picked with a probability proportional to the log of the rank
  1. A model of document utility, describing how a user derives utility from individual
    relevant documents
    • In case of DCG: user gains something from each relevant document, proportional to its relevance degree
  1. A utility accumulation model that describes how a user accumulates utility in the course
    of browsing
    • In case of DCG: the user gains from all of the relevant documents from ranks 111﻿ through kkk﻿ but discounting the later retrieved relevant documents

A significance test is based on:

  1. Null hypothesis H0H_0H0​﻿: no difference between A and B [changes we see are by chance]
  1. Alternative hypothesis: B is better than A

A significant test enables us to reject the null hypothesis in favour of the alternative hypothesis (B is better than A) [reasonably sure that H0H_0H0​﻿ is false, but we could be wrong]

  • Otherwise B might not be better than A [we can’t conclude anything]

The power of a test is the probability that the test will reject the null hypothesis correctly

  • Power=P(Reject H0​∣H1​ is true)\text{Power}=P(\text{Reject }H_0​∣H_1​ \text{ is true})Power=P(Reject H0​​∣H1​​ is true)﻿
  • Increasing the number of queries in the experiment (realizations of the experiment) also increases power of a test

  1. Compute the effectiveness measure for every query in both experiments
  1. Compute a test statistic based on a comparison of the effectiveness measures for each query [two sets of effectiveness measures]
    1. The test statistic is used to compute a p-value, which is the probability that a test statistic value at least that extreme (≥\ge≥﻿) could be observed if the null hypothesis were true
    1. Small p-values suggest that the null hypothesis may be false
    1. The null hypothesis (no difference) is rejected in favour of the alternative hypothesis (B is better than A) if p-value<α\text{p-value} < \alphap-value<α﻿ (significance level) → decide a priori
      • α=P(Reject H0∣H0 is true)α=P(\text{Reject }H_0∣H_0 \text{ is true})α=P(Reject H0​∣H0​ is true)﻿

  • Single-tailed test: B is better than A? μB>μB\mu_B> \mu _BμB​>μB​﻿
