  • Energy harvesting enables batteryless IoT devices to operate without external power sources → may not always available, so in general it keeps a battery
  • Plan the energy consuption for today based on the quantity of energy harvesting predicted

  1. Elimination of battery replacement costs
  1. Reduced downtime and maintenance
  1. Longer lifetimes (potentially unbounded)
  1. Environmental friendly

Energy Conservation

Needs a preliminary analysis to study how the energy consumption is distributed

  • Not general, we need to make this analysis with each device
  • In Idle I can still receiving immediatly
  • Why sleep rather the turn off? Longer timer to turn up

In cellular communications, trasmission is more consuming than reception since the distance is big to reach the base station, the device must transmit at higher power.

  • In IoT this is not the case since the distance is very small and the power emitted by the antenna is very low
  • In IoT most of the energy is used by the electronics: microcontrollers, sensors, signal processing, and especially the radio circuitry

Goal is to reduce as much as possible the radio activity → sleep/off mode

We have 3 main approaches:

  1. Data driven approach: reduce as much as possible the amount of data to transmit to the final destination
  1. Mobility-based approach
  1. Duty-cycling approach when the device is inactive you put it in sleep mode. Reduce the percentage of activity respect to the whole cycle

1) Data Driven Approach

a. Data Reduction

Once the sampling rate of the sensors have been defined, try to reduce the amount of data to be transferred

Data Aggregation

Aggregate more data from different packets into a single one

  • Reduce the overhead due to control information (headers)

  1. At MAC-layer: combines multiple network packets into a single MAC frame
    • This solution is independent from other layers, we have no knowledge about network topology and it is application independent
  1. At cluster head node multiple messages are aggregated
  1. Tree based aggregation: each node send data to its parent that accumulate them and send a single msg to its parent, until root (sink)

Application-aware Data Aggregation → data centricity, not all msg are forwarded to the sink

  • Depends on the application domain cosidered
  • Can be exploit both in tree-based and cluster-based networks

Aggregation factor: is the ratio between the number of bits to be transmitted with aggregation and the number of bits to be transmitted without aggregation

  • NNN﻿ is the number of messages to be aggregated

Data Prediction

Derive a model of an observed phenomenon, based on initial measurements

  • Instead of reporting all data to the sink, only send the model parameters
    • save energy by sending less data
  • Periodic measurements to understand if the model is still accurate, in case update the parameters
    • If the past sensed values (k samples) falls within an application-dependent tolerance, then the model is considered valid
    • Otherwise, the source node may transmit the sampled data and/or start a model update procedure involving the sink as well

There are two instances of a model in the network, one residing at the sink and the other at source nodes

  • The model at the sink can be used to answer queries without requiring any communication, thus reducing the energy consumption

Data Compression

This scheme involves encoding information at nodes that generate data and decoding it at the sink.

  • There are different algorithms to compress data (eg. Huffman code)
  • Can be lossless or lossy compression

Application requirements to study optimal sampling rates

  • Energy-efficient data acquisition techniques decrease also the number of communications as well

Radio is really fast to wake up, instead the sensor has a larger warm up time (in the mean time almost random values)

Hierarchical Sensing

Using different sensors with different power consumption and resolution properties

  • The low power sensors are always active (typically the less accurate) → when something happen (eg. intrusion) wake up the others (high resolution)

Two different categories:

  1. Multi-Scale Sensing: Low-resolution wide area sensors are used to identify areas of interests, High resolution sensors are, then, switched on for more accurate measurements

  1. Triggered sensing: Low-power low resolution sensors trigger high-power and high-accuracy sensors
    • The two sensors do not measure the same thing

Adative Sampling Algorithm (ASA)

Adapts the sampling rate to the dynamics of the phenomenon under monitoring

  • exploits the temporal correlation and spatial correlation
  • Change the sampling frequency with the dynamics of the signal (if it change quickly sample a lot)

Based on the Nyquist Theorem

  • It exploits temporal correlation in this case

  1. Dynamic computation of maximum frequency → can change over time
  1. Sample the signal with twice the maximum frequency

Frequency Change Detection: CUSUM algorithm (cumulative sum)

  1. Define two alternative hypothesis FupF_{up}Fup​﻿, FdownF_{down}Fdown​﻿ for the maximum frequency of the signal during the operational life
  1. If the current maximum frequency FcurrF_{curr}Fcurr​﻿ of the signal (W samples) during the operational life is closer to FupF_{up}Fup​﻿ or FdownF_{down}Fdown​﻿ then FmaxF_{max}Fmax​﻿ for h consecutive samples, a change is detected in the maximum frequency of the signal
    1. Inside the two threshold thupth_{up}thup​﻿, thdownth_{down}thdown​﻿ is just fluctuation of the signal → no changes
  1. A new sampling frequency is defined
Algorithm ASA(c, δ, h, w)
// c -> sampling factor, c >= 2 (c=2 if the estimation F_max is perfect)
// h -> number of times to be above, below thr. to identify a change 
//      critical to the robustness of the algorithm (false positives (h=2) vs. less promptness to detect changes h=300)
// δ -> proportional to the size of the band
// W -> number of samples of the windows.
//      critical to the accuracy of the algotithm (low value -> low energy consumption, not accurate)

1. Store the first W samples in the Dataset.
2. Compute F_max from the Dataset and set F_c = c * F_max.
3. Define:
	 - F_up = min{(1 + δ) * F_max, F_c / 2}
	 - F_down = (1 - δ) * F_max
4. Initialize:
   - h1 = 0  // how many the current estimation is above the upper th
   - h2 = 0  // below bottom threshold
   - i = W + 1  // index of the sample I'm considering

5. while (true) do:
   6. Acquire the i-th sample and add it to the Dataset.
   7. Compute the current maximum frequency F_curr from Dataset(i - W + 1, i)  // last W sample
   8. if ( |F_curr - F_up| < |F_curr - F_max| ) then:
        h1 = h1 + 1, h2 = 0
   9. else if ( |F_curr - F_down| < |F_curr - F_max| ) then:
        h2 = h2 + 1, h1 = 0
  10. else:
        h1 = 0, h2 = 0
  11. if (h1 > h) or (h2 > h) then:
        F_c = c * F_curr
        F_max = F_curr
        F_up = min{(1 + δ) * F_max, F_c / 2}
        F_down = (1 - δ) * F_max

Model Based Sensing

Learn the spatio-temporal relationship among measurements and use this knowledge to make the sensing process energy efficient

A model of the phenomenon to be monitored is built and updated dynamically, based on measurements from sensor nodes. Then the sensor node decides whether:

  1. To acquire a new sample through a measurement
  1. To estimate this new sample, with the desired accuracy, through the model → probabilistic methods, regression models, ML models and so on

First acquire data to derive the model, than use it to predict some data (virtual sensor) and sample the environment sometimes → check if the model is still valid (accordance with the model), in case update it if its violated for more then h points

  • Reduce the energy consumed by the sensor and by the radio

Data Reduction Considerations

Just reducing the amount of data (Data Reduction) does not necessarily result in energy consumption reduction

  • Transmitting a message requires approximately the same energy, irrespective of the message size
  • They are not proportional, a 99% suppression of data results in only a tripling of lifetime
  • Trasmitting data isn’t the only power consumption in radio:
    1. Idle listening
    1. Overhead for the routing protocols (update paths, must trasmit control messages)
    1. Techniques that reduce redundancies needs reliable communication (reduce correlations) → cannot recover msg
      • Not a valid assumption since they are low power and lossy networks
      • Here if the packet is damaged I need to retrasmit

2. Duty Cycling

Node components are put in sleep/off mode when not needed.

  • It consists in cycling between active and sleep states
  • A duty cycle is the fraction of time an entity (in this case radio) spends in an active state as a proportion of the total time considered

Two strategies, not exclusives:

  1. Topology Control:
    • Exploits network redundancy → increases the cost of number of devices. But it’s not the only cost source, it’s better to have redundancy instead of replacing the device individually every time it fails
    • Selects the minimum set of nodes that guarantees connectivity
    • All the other nodes are kept in sleep mode to save energy. This increases the network lifetime by a factor depending on the degree of redundancy
  1. Power Management: Exploits idle periods in communication
    • Switches off the radio during inactive periods
    • Extends network lifetime significantly

a. Topology Control

Goal: Find out the minimum subset of nodes to activate in order to ensure network connectivity. We can choose:

  1. Few active nodes: distance between neighboring nodes is high → increased packet loss
    • Higher transmit power and reduced spatial reuse
  1. Too many active nodes: unnecessary energy consuption
    • at worst nodes may interfere with one another → congesting the channel

This is an optimization problem:

  1. Given Node position and trasmission range
  1. Calculate the minimum number of nodes to activate in order to ensure connectivity

But is not working in practice → assumptions like constant trasmission range over time and circular reachability

Connectivity Driven

ASCENT → Decision about joining the network based on local measurements by the node

  • Each node measures the number of neighbors and packet loss locally and makes an informed decision to join the network topology or to sleep by turning its radio off

The node states can be:

  1. Active → nodes are part of the topology and forward data packets
  1. Test → nodes collect network measurements and forward packets
  1. Passive → no message forwaring but local measures. They can receive or being asleep. They do not forward any packet

  • LTLTLT﻿: loss threshold
  • NTNTNT﻿: neighbors threshold
  • loss>loss T0loss > loss \space T_0 loss>loss T0​﻿ (loss increase instead of decreasing) → more collisions
  • TPTPTP﻿: time after which if nothing is sense goes to sleep
  • TSTSTS﻿: time after which the node wake up to perform local measurements

An active node may send help messages to solicit passive neighbors to become active if it is experiencing message loss.

A node that joins the network (test state) or that change its state sends an announcement message

  • The process continues until the number of active nodes is such that the experienced message loss is below a pre defined application-dependent threshold.
  • The process will re-start when some future network event (e.g. a node failure) or a change in the environmental conditions causes an increase in the message loss.

Then it may decide to become active forever after some time (other algorithms may consider to go back from test).

  • adaptive → probabilistic models to determine the best parameters/thresholds runtime

b. Power Management

In general means that the radio (or a component or the whole sensor) alternate between sleep and active mode → exploits idle period in communication

  • Switches off the radio during inactive periods → extends the network lifetime significantly
  • Duty cycles of some percent are quite common in WSNs

Can be implanted in different place of the layers stack:

  1. General sleep/wakeup schemes: implemented in higher layers
    • On demand, Scheduled rendezvous, Asynchronous
    • In reality that could be implemented in all layer
  1. Low duty-cycle MAC protocols: implemented at the MAC sublayer

General Sleep/Wakeup schemes

If always sleeping we cannot receive (meanwhile trasmitting is not a problem, just wake up the radio)

When should a node wake up for communicating with its neighbors?

  1. When another node wants to communicate with it (on demand) → data radio always sleeping and activated on demand by a wake-up message
  1. At the same time as its neighbors (scheduled rendez-vous) → the two nodes agree on the time on which the communication will happen
  1. Whenever it wants (Asynchronous) → if I want to receive I will wake-up the radio

I) On Demand

Wake-up Radio → is a second radio in order to receive and ask the data radio of another node to power on

  • We could use the same radio with two different frequencies

  • It must be really energy efficient (always on), otherwise it’s counterproductive → results in a smaller communication range
  • The secondary radio introduces an additional cost of the device

The sources detects an event, then we will send a wake up message to all the hop until we deliver it to the final destination.

  • delay of the wakes-up

I can perform the two thing in parallel → wake-up mine data radio and simultaneously sent a wake-up msg to the next node

WuTX/RX → wake up radio

WuS → wake up signal

False wake-ups and interference → when WuTx tries to wake-up a node, it will trigger all the nodes in the neighborhood causing significant energy waste

  • Node addressing to trigger only the intended node
  • Interference and noise that can result in erroneous wake-ups: must be filtered
    • Only basic modulation techniques can be used → otherwise more cost and energy
    • A preamble can be used to differentiate noise from a valid WuS, thus avoiding false wake-ups (costly)

WuR ideally should have the same range of the data radio → but in practice since the low energy the maximum distance in which the communication is possible is reduced (because is all based on the wake-up radio)

  • Most WuR designs target tens of meters of communication range
  • Could be improved through directional antennas

Data Rate requirements

  1. High data rate → shorter wakeup period because message is trasmitted quicker and so less energy waste
    • A high data rate is not strictly required by the WuR, since only a few bytes of data are required
  1. Low data rate → longer range and better communication reliability

II) Scheduled Rendez-Vous

Nodes wake up according to a wakeup schedule, and remain active for a short time interval to communicate with their neighbors. Then, they go to sleep until the next rendez-vous time.

  • Assume a tree topology → root node is the sink

Master node to which all other nodes will synch their clock → periodic clock msg because there is a drift between nodes

  • Syncronization consume energy
  1. Fully synchronized scheme → clock synch at the beginning, then sleep until the next epoch (simplest method)
    • Problem of syncronize all the clocks → send a msg at each epoch, but it consume energy
    • The duty cycle should be the lower as possible, in order to guarantee energy saving
    • This method increase the collision probability over the network since all will communicate in the same period
  1. Fixed Staggered Scheme → only a node subset is active at the same time
    • Layer by layer will be activated, from leafs to root (parent child talk intervals) → suitable for data aggregation
    • Obviously, the active parts of nodes belonging to adjacent levels must be partially overlapped to allow nodes to communicate with their children
    • With fewer collisions and less data to transmit using this scheme, it is possible to have a smaller communication window
  1. Adaptive Staggered Scheme → not fixed up time, because it wasn’t so effective
    • It depends on the number of children, network traffic, channel conditions, data to be trasmitted
    • I need coordination between layers on the next epoch activity time period → special messages (sleep coordination)
    • Perfect for energy saving, but increses complexity of syncronization and new messagges

III) Asynchronous

Asynchronous schemes enable each node to wake up, transmit whenever they desire, and then return to a sleeping state

  1. Burden on the receiver → When the sender has a msg to transmit, it periodically send a special short message to announce it
    • Periodically the receiver will wake up and receive the announcement from the sender and become ready to communicate
    • In order to be sure to receive at least one entire announcement:

      Trx=2Ton+TidleT_{rx} = 2T_{on}+T_{idle}Trx​=2Ton​+Tidle​﻿

    • High energy consumption since a lot of recevier in the network
  1. Burden on the sender: the sender send a longer announcement just once → most of the energy is consumed by the sender since they must stay wake up longer
    • Better then the first method, because the sender are less (maybe just 1)
    • Very good if the msgs to be sent are sporadically
    • The receiver wakes up periodically (periodically listening) for a very short time: if there is a trasmission, the receiver remain active until the end of the msg

Async ex1) Low Power Listening (LPL)

Low power Listening (TinyOS) → burden on the sender

Sender uses a long preamble before each packet to wake up the receiver → burden on the sender

  • implemented by TinyOS

Data packet is very small with respect to the preamble

  • when node2 wakes up, it sense the preamble and stay active until the packet is received

Every receiver remains active for the rest of the preamble

  • inside the preamble should send the adress for the destination

Async ex2) ContikiMAC

The idea is to optimize the previous protocol in order to reduce the total energy consumed by the two nodes

  • The sender doesn’t send a long preamble and a small data packet but it sends continously the data packet → until a link layer ack is received

If the receiver senses a packet trasmission directed to it, the receiver stays awake to receive the next entire packet

  • Reduce the time the receiver must stay awake and also the size of the preable of the sender (since there is a small delay between packets in order to receive the ack)
    • Energy saving in both receiver and sender
  • Two clear channel assesment (CCA) consecutively from the receiver → I get two different constraint on timing
    • needs to handle what happen if one fails and the other no

Two techniques to additionally save energy:

  1. Fast sleep optimization
    1. If a silence period is not detected before tlt_ltl​﻿ (maximum packet trasmission time) the receiver goes back to sleep
      • Allows potential receivers go to sleep quickly if the CCA woke up due to spurious radio
    1. If the silence period is longer than tit_iti​﻿ there is no trasmission (false positive) → receiver goes back to sleep
  1. Trasmission Phase lock → the sender, based on the receiver ack, knows the next active period and its frequency
    • Modify the start of the communication nearest as possible to the active cycle of the receiver
    • The sender save energy, for the receiver doesn’t change anything

3. Mobility Based Approach

It reduces power consumption by leveraging mobile components to optimize data collection and transmission.

  • The funneling effect occurs in multi-hop IoT networks when a large volume of data from multiple nodes converges toward a static sink through a few key relay nodes → this will reduce it

  1. Mobile sink approach: a mobile sink travels within the network to collect data from sensor nodes
  1. Mobile relay approach: an intermediate node that moves between static sensors and a sink to facilitate data transmission
