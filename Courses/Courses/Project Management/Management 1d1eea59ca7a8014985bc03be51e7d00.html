Cost Classification

Purposes:

  1. Assigning costs to cost objects (entity to which I want to give a cost)
    • Direct cost and indirect cost
  1. Preparing financial statements
  1. Predicting cost behavior in response to changes in activity
  1. Making decisions

1. Assigning Costs to Cost Objects

Manufacturing Costs

Direct costs

  • Costs that can be easily and conveniently traced to a unit of product or other cost object

Example:

  • Direct Material → objects from the BOM (bill of material for the product) → will become part of the product
    • A radio installed in an automobile
  • Direct labor costs are those labor costs that can be easily traced to individual units of product → timetables of workers (eg software company with a lot of projects)
    • wages paid to automobile assembly workers (directly on the object)

Indirect costs

  • Manufacturing overhead includes all manufacturing costs except direct material and direct labor
    • These costs cannot be readily traced to finished products
  • Example: manufacturing overhead
    • Lubrification of the machines, security guards, property tax, insurance

  • Conversion cost refers to the costs incurred to convert raw materials into finished goods.

Non Manufacturing Costs - Period Cost

2. Cost for financial statements (eg. Balance Sheet)

Consist of

  1. product costs
  1. period costs

Product costs

Product costs → includes all costs that are involed in acquiring or making a product

  • until a product is in inventory

For manufacturing companies, product costs include:

  • Raw materials: includes any materials that go into the final product.
  • Work in process: consists of units of product that are only partially complete and will require further work before they are ready for sale to the customer.
  • Finished goods costs: consists of completed units of product that have not yet been sold to customers.


When products move through the stages of production, the costs are transferred from raw materials to work in process to finished goods. Once a product is sold, its cost is moved from the inventory account (finished goods) to the Cost of Goods Sold

3. Cost classification for predicting cost behaviour

Variable Costs → cost that varies with direct proportion to changes in the level of activity

  • variable cost per unit is constant

Cost driver

  1. machine hours
  1. miles driven
  1. labor hours
  1. direct materials

Fixed cost → remains constat regardless of changes in the level of the activity

  • If expressed on a per unit basis, the average fixed cost per unit varies inversely with change in activity (hyperbolic function)

  1. rent
  1. salaries (if fixed staff)
  1. insurance
  • discretionary eg. cut the compensation of the CEO, the consultants, the library access to journals, advertising
  • Committed → fire people in europe

  • curvature imply different returns rate

Mixed costs

A mixed cost contains both variable and fixed elements.

  • for example utilities costs (bolletta della luce)

4. Cost classification for making decisions

Decisions involve choosing between alternatives. The goal of making decisions is to identify those costs that are either relevant or irrelevant to the decision

Differential Costs

Differential costs (or incremental costs) are the difference in cost between any two alternatives.

  • A difference in revenue between two alternatives is called differential revenue.
  • Can be either fixed or variable

Eg the payment of a consultants is not differential, since it doesnt change if you make product A or B. Meanwhile if you change the material may be differential

Sunk Costs

Sunk costs have already been incurred and cannot be changed now or in the future.
These costs should be ignored when making decisions.

  • already pay the consultant for deciding if to produce A or B → useless to consider when choosing for product A or B
  • The amount spent on the past that cannot be recovered → doesn't affect the future

Opportunity Cost

The potential benefit that is given up when one alternative is selected over another.

  • For students: What is the opportunity cost you incur by attending class?
    • Opportunity cost of not working
