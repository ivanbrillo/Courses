  • Return on Asset ROA=EBITFA+CAROA = \frac{EBIT}{FA + CA}ROA=FA+CAEBIT​﻿ (over total assets)
    • ROA shows how efficiently the company uses all its assets to generate profits. A higher ROA means better efficiency
  • Return on Investment ROI=EBITOF+LTLROI = \frac{EBIT}{OF+LTL}ROI=OF+LTLEBIT​﻿ (over capital employed)
    • ROI measures how effectively the total capital employed (equity + long-term debt) is used to generate operating profits. Higher ROI means better capital productivity
  • Return on equity ROE=EATOFROE = \frac{EAT}{OF}ROE=OFEAT​﻿
    • ROE tells how much profit is generated for each unit of equity. It's important for shareholders since it shows return on their investment.

Liquidity Ratios

  • Current Ratio CR=CACLCR = \frac{CA}{CL}CR=CLCA​﻿
    • Measures the ability to pay short-term debts with short-term assets. A ratio above 1 is generally safe.
  • Quick Ratio QR=CLCashQR = \frac{CL}{\text{Cash}}QR=CashCL​﻿
    • Measures the ability to pay current liabilities immediately using the most liquid assets. It's a stricter test than the current ratio.

Solvency Ratios

  • Equity ratio ER=OFLTL+CLER = \frac{OF}{LTL+CL}ER=LTL+CLOF​﻿ (over total liabilities)
    • Measures the proportion of total liabilities funded by owner’s equity.
    • Higher ER → more stable and less reliant on debt.
  • Debt to equity ratio DER=LTLOFDER = \frac{LTL}{OF}DER=OFLTL​﻿
    • Compares long-term debt to owner’s equity.
    • High DER → higher financial risk (more debt than equity)

    • Tells how much dividend income a shareholder earns for each dollar invested in the stock

Cash Flow Ratio

  • Free Cash Flow FCF=Operating Cash Flow−Capital ExpendituresFCF = \text{Operating Cash Flow} - \text{Capital Expenditures}FCF=Operating Cash Flow−Capital Expenditures﻿
    • This represents the cash a company generates after accounting for cash outflows to support operations and maintain capital assets.
  • FCFTR\frac{FCF}{TR}TRFCF​﻿ (over total revenue)
    • This measures how efficiently a company converts its sales into free cash flow. It shows how many dollars of free cash are generated per dollar of revenue
  • FCFFA+CA\frac{FCF}{FA+CA}FA+CAFCF​﻿
    • This indicates how efficiently a company's assets generate free cash flow. It shows the return on assets in terms of cash generation

  • Free Cash Flow to Equity FCFE=FCF−Interest ExpensesOFFCFE = \frac{FCF- \text{Interest Expenses}}{OF}FCFE=OFFCF−Interest Expenses​﻿
    • This measures how much cash is available to equity shareholders relative to their investment. It indicates the cash return generated for shareholders after servicing debt obligations
