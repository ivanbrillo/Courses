Summary

External Factors & Strategic Environment

  • General Environment: Broad, macro-level forces (e.g., interest rates, exchange rates) that managers can't control.
  • Task Environment: Industry-specific factors (e.g., competitors, customers) that managers can influence.

PESTEL Model

Categorizes environmental influences into:

  • Political, Economic, Sociocultural, Technological, Ecological, Legal factors.

Firm vs. Industry Effects

  • Industry effects: Common to all firms (e.g., structure, barriers).
  • Firm effects: Result from a firm’s unique strategies and actions—more impactful on performance.

Five Forces Model

Analyzes industry profitability based on:

  1. Threat of Entry
  1. Power of Suppliers
  1. Power of Buyers
  1. Threat of Substitutes
  1. Rivalry Among Competitors

→ Stronger forces mean lower profit potential.

Other Key Concepts

  • Types of Competition: Ranges from perfect competition to monopoly.
  • Strategic Commitments: Long-term, costly decisions that affect rivalry.
  • Exit Barriers: Factors making it hard to leave an industry.
  • Complements: Products/services that increase value (e.g., Apple ecosystem).
  • Co-opetition: Competitors cooperating strategically.
  • Industry Dynamics: Acknowledges industry change (e.g., innovation, mergers).
  • Industry Convergence: Different industries merging due to tech (e.g., media).
  • Strategic Groups: Firms with similar strategies within an industry (e.g., low-cost vs. full-service airlines)
