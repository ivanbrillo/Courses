Project Managing

  • everything is projectize now a day

A project is any series of activities that:

  1. have a specific objective with focus of creating a business value
  1. time bounded (start and finish)
  1. have funding limits (budget)
  1. consume human and nonhuman resources (people, money, assets)

Project Management is the planning, controlling of company resources for a relativly short term objective that has been established to complete

3 main approaches:

  1. waterfall
  1. agile way
  1. hybrid

Traditional (WaterFall)

predict how things will unfold

  • heavy upfront analysis and documentation about problem and solution
  • Plan all the steps to takes (eg. machine designing)

  • too rigid
  • commit to the solution too early → cannot change, iterations, feedback from customers
  • focused more on process, rather then product

Successful projects:

  • LHC at CERN → detailed documentation
  • Burj Khalifa → predictability

Agile

focused small increment

  • do not plan the whole thing, just a little, then do it and then check
  • there is no need of a formal project manager

  • easy to get distracted
  • project doesn’t have a finite end
  • constant interaction takes more time and energy from everyone → also the customer must be involved

Agile manifesto

  • uncovering a better ways to develop software
  • better have working software rather then comprehensive documentation

Success

  • Google seach engine
  • amazon
  • Spotify

Hybrid Approach

Agile approach on the front to explore potential solutions and a predictive approach to build that solution

  • Agile cannot work for big team sizes
  • can handle complex project structures, with uncertain requirements
  • could alternate agile with predictive phases

Success:

  • Ubisoft
  • Tesla
    • gigafactory → waterfall
    • software → agile

Best Practice

  • Actions or activities undertaken by the company or individuals that lead to a sustained competitive advantage in project management
  • Best practices can be learned from both successes and failures

Proven Practice

  • A best practice begins with an idea that there is a technique, process, method, or activity that can be more effective at delivering an outcome than any other approach and provides us with the desired outcome with fewer problems and unforeseen complication
  • Once this idea has been proven to be effective, we normally integrate the best practice into our processes so that it becomes a standard way of doing business → “Proven practice”

2. Define Activities: WBS

Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

  • A hierarchical decomposition of the total scope of work to be carried out
  • Complicated task → smaller tasks until they cannot be further subdivided

Scope creep are additional scope or requirements that are accepted without adjusting schedule, budget, or resource needs.

  • Use a change control system where all changes are evaluated for the potential value they
    bring to the project and the potential resources, time, and budget needed to realize the
    potential value. To be presented to the project governance body, product owner, or executive
    sponsor for formal approval

Out of scope: an element that is not contained in the WBS and not a part of the project

Needs to find a good stop for decomposition

  • smaller work are more accurate to schedule and to control costs
  • creating a really complex decomposition with 100s of entries can be so complex to analyze

3. Sequence Activities

Dependencies are those things that influence which activities must be performed first

(Activity B cannot + second term + before A + second term )

Types of dependencies

  • Mandatory: cannot be broken
  • Discretionary: are not always true – usually result of best practises
  • External: are outside of the project’s control and scope
  • Internal: those that the team can control

Types of contraints

  • Technical constraints: one task (the successor) requires output from another (the predecessor) before work can begin on it
  • Management constraints
  • Interproject constraints: deliverables from one project are needed by another project
  • Date constraints

  • Lead: amount of time a successor acrivity can be advanced with respect to predecessor
    • allows an acceleration
  • Lag: amount of time a successor activity will be delayed with respect to a predecessor activity.
    • Imposes a delay of the successor activity. Introduce risk, especially in JIT (just in time) inventory management

4. Estimating Activity Durations

  1. Analogous estimating: uses values, or attributes, of a previous project that are similar to the current project.
  1. Parametric estimating: uses a statistical relationship between relevant historical data and other
    variables
  1. Bottom-up estimating: The work within the activity is decomposed into more detail. These estimates are then aggregated into a total quantity for each of the activity’s durations
  1. 3-point estimating → Helps define an approximate range for an activity’s duration
    1. Triangular distribution … (optimistic + most likely + pessimistic) / 3
    1. Beta distribution … (optimistic + 4 * most likely + pessimistic) / 6

probability of bad outcome is higher → skew

Assuming that the paths are independents of one another, the probability that the entire project will be on time can be calculated by multiplying these probabilities together

  • To complete a project by a specified time require that all the paths in the project’s network needs to be completed by the specified time

5. Develop Schedule

Process of analyzing activity sequences, durations, resource requirements, and schedule constraints to create a schedule model for project execution and monitoring and controlling

  • Gantt Charts

Project schedule network diagrams: two types

Critical path is dynamic

  • Near critical path: path close in duration to the critical path.
  • The closer the near-critical and critical path are, the more risk the project has

Free float ≤ total float

Resource optimization = adjust the start and finish dates of activities to adjust planned resource use to be equal to or less than resource availability

  • Resource leveling: start and finish dates are adjusted based on resource constraints with the goal
    of balancing the demand for resources with the available supply.
    • Resource leveling can often cause the original critical path to change.
    • Available float is used for leveling resources.
    • Consequently, the critical path through the project schedule may change
  • Resource smoothing: adjusts the activities of a schedule model such that the requirements for
    resources on the project do not exceed certain predefined resource limit

Techniques used to shorten or accelerate the schedule duration without reducing the project scope

  1. Fast tracking: activities or phases normally done in sequence are performed in parallel a portion of their duration for at least
  1. Crashing: shorten the schedule duration for the least incremental cost by adding resources

6. Control Schedule

Key performance indicators (KPIs): quantifiable measures to evaluate the success of a project

  1. Leading indicators
    • predict changes or trends in the project
    • can reduce performance risk by identifying potential performance variances before they cross the tolerance threshold
  1. Lagging indicators
    • measure project deliverables or events
    • reflect past performance conditions

  • Baseline schedule = version of the schedule that has been approved
  • Current schedule = current period version of the schedule based on actual work and forecast
  • Variance = difference between the estimated effort in the baseline and the actual effort performed by the team

Common pitfalls of measuring

  1. Hawthorne effect: the act of measuring something influences behavior.
  1. Vanity metrics: measure that shows data but does not provide useful information for making decisions.
  1. Confirmation bias: look for and see information that supports our preexisting point of view.
  1. Correlation versus causation. confusing the correlation of two variables with the idea
    that one causes the other.
