Five Criteria for Successful Segmentation

For successful segmentation, a company needs to ensure their target segments satisfy all these criteria

  • Effective: represents segmentation that effectively meets strategic goals and addresses customer needs
  • Measurable: indicates that segments should be quantifiable and measurable to track performance
  • Accessible: refers to segments being reachable through marketing communications and distribution channels
  • Actionable: segments should be specific enough to develop actionable marketing strategies and programs
  • Profitable: segments should be large or valuable enough to profitably serve.

Target Marketing

Target marketing refers to the choice of specific segments to serve, and is a key element in marketing strategy.

  • Organizations need to evaluate the segments to decide which ones to serve using the five criteria for successful segmentation.
  • For example, the Coca-Cola brand Powerade conducted research across five European countries and identified seven consumer segments whose attitudes and needs varied significantly and chose to focus on the ‘true sportsman’ segment

Strategies

  • Undifferentiated Marketing
    • No segmentation is used - the company treats the whole market as having similar needs
    • Mass marketing approach that focuses on commonalities rather than differences
  • Differentiated Marketing
    • Multiple marketing mixes (1, 2, 3) are developed for different market segments
    • The company recognizes distinct customer groups (segment differences) and creates tailored approaches for each
    • Example: A clothing brand with separate lines for men, women, and children
  • Focused Marketing
    • The company specializes in serving one specific segment very well
    • Also called concentrated marketing or niche marketing
    • Example: A luxury watchmaker focusing exclusively on high-net-worth individuals
  • Customized Marketing
    • Individual marketing mixes are created for individual customers
    • The highest level of segmentation, treating each customer as a unique segment
    • Also known as one-to-one marketing or personalized marketing
    • Example: Custom tailoring services or enterprise B2B solutions designed for specific clients

Process of Personalization

In the context of market segmentation, this represents the evolution toward customized marketing:

  1. Data Collection and Interactions: The cycle begins with capturing customer interactions and external data (shown in the outer red ring), which forms the foundation of personalization.
  1. Processing Stage: Raw customer data is analyzed to create detailed customer profiles. This goes beyond traditional segmentation by focusing on individual preferences, behaviors, and characteristics.
  1. Customization Stage: Using these profiles, marketing outputs are specifically tailored to individual customers rather than broader segments.
  1. Delivery Stage: The personalized marketing outputs are delivered to individual customers through appropriate channels.
  1. Continuous Feedback Loop: The customer's response generates new interaction data, which feeds back into the system, creating a self-improving cycle.

In modern marketing, this personalization approach has become increasingly feasible through digital technologies, data analytics, and AI, allowing companies to move beyond broad segmentation to truly individualized marketing at scale

Positioning

Refers to the act of designing the company’s offering, so that it occupies a meaningful and distinct position in the target consumers mind

  • Kellogg’s has positioned its Special K offering for females and its Bran Flakes for males

Deciding what position to occupy in the market requires consideration of three variables:

  1. The customers – what attributes matter to them;
  1. The competitors – a differential advantage that ideally cannot be easily matched;
  1. The company – unique sustainable attributes

  1. Clarity:
    • Positioning must be clearly defined and easily understood by the target audience
    • The unique value proposition should be immediately apparent
      • Avoids confusion about what the brand or product stands for
  1. Consistency:
    • The positioning message must be maintained consistently across all marketing channels and touchpoints
    • Reinforces brand identity through repeated, coherent messaging
  1. Credibility:
    • Positioning claims must be believable and authentic to the target audience
    • The position taken should align with company capabilities and values
  1. Competitiveness:
    • Positioning must differentiate the brand/product from competitors
    • Highlights unique benefits or attributes that competition doesn't offer

The Perceptual Map

The perceptual map is a useful tool for determining the position of a brand in the marketplace.

The key steps in producing a perceptual map are:

  1. Identify a set of competing brands;
  1. Identify the important attributes consumers use when choosing between brands;
  1. Conduct quantitative marketing research where consumers score each brand on all key attributes;
  1. Plot brands on a two-dimensional map

Repositioning

Repositioning involves changing:

  1. the target markets;
  1. the differential advantage (how your product is perceived compared to competitors)
  1. or both

Types of repostioning

  • Image Repositioning
    • Keep the product and target market the same
    • Change only the image or perception of the product
  • Intangible repositioning
    • Retain the same product or the same market and change the other
    • Example: When Nintendo Wii repositioned gaming consoles to target families and casual players rather than hardcore gamers
  • Tangible Repositioning
    • Change both the product and the target market
    • The most comprehensive form of repositioning
    • Example: When IBM shifted from primarily selling hardware to offering business consulting and software services, changing both what they sold and who they sold to
