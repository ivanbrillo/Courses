Abstract models

  1. Synchronous Model: In the synchronous model, all processes are assumed to operate synchronously, driven by a global clock. This model simplifies reasoning about communication but is unrealistic as there is no global clock in real distributed systems.
    1. The time to execute each step of a process has known lower and upper bounds.
    1. Each message transmitted over a channel is received within a known bounded time.
    1. Each process has a local clock whose drift rate from real time has a known bound.
  1. Asynchronous Model: In the asynchronous model, no assumptions are made about process synchronization.
    • Message delivery delays are considered unbounded. This model is more realistic but also more complex to manage.

Possible behaviours of send/receive primitives:

  • Blocking - at execution, the process possibly blocks to wait for the operation to be completed
  • Non-blocking - the operation is just "fired" and the process keeps on executing; it requires buffering of messages.

The implementations of these primitives requires use of mailboxes (queues)

  • use semantic in pair may lead to deadlock (receive/send)
